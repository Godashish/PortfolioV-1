01 — Profile

Where the policy meets the ledger

My work sits between two things that rarely talk to each other: the institutions that allocate development finance, and the evidence about whether that allocation works.

At the Deposit & Credit Guarantee Fund I design and monitor guarantee schemes for small enterprises and smallholder farmers, contributing to the World Bank-supported Sustainable and Inclusive Finance Project and a UNDP-partnered agricultural guarantee scheme. That work involves regulatory analysis and close coordination with Nepal Rastra Bank and the Ministry of Finance.

Alongside it, I publish. My research applies time-series econometrics and machine learning to questions about climate finance, monetary policy spillovers from India, and the determinants of Nepal's stock market. Seven years of prior audit and analysis work across Nepal and India gave me the operational grounding for it.

Research interests

  • Institutional governance of climate transition
  • Comparative financial sector policy in South Asia
  • Sustainable finance regulation and green taxonomies
  • Macroeconomic policy in developing economies

    with Swachhal Sapkota, Janapriya Multiple Campus · SSRN · Posted 8 Jun 2026 · 11 pp. Under peer review

03 — Applied research

Things I built

Climate Vulnerability and Economic Resilience

A country clustering analysis, with a focus on South Asia

Are the most climate-vulnerable countries actually prepared for the transition? This study clusters 60+ countries on vulnerability against economic readiness using K-Means, then models readiness with Random Forest and Gradient Boosting regressors to find what actually predicts it. Built on World Bank WDI and ND-GAIN data, and deployed as a Streamlit dashboard so the clusters can be explored rather than just read about.

  • Four clusters Crisis Zone, Vulnerable & Underprepared, Emerging & Transitioning, and Resilient & Ready.
  • GDP per capita and literacy are the strongest predictors of climate readiness — not renewable energy adoption or forest coverage.
  • South Asia splits sharply Nepal and Afghanistan fall in the Crisis Zone while India and the Maldives score higher on readiness.
  • Institutions appear to matter Rwanda, Nepal, and India each outperform what their economic fundamentals predict.
